# Diagnostic tension and fragility

## Research question

> Are diagnostic questions refinements owned by each Document, or do they reveal a distinct transversal documentary responsibility that is not yet modeled?

## Observation

Across multiple Document types, useful semantic pressure repeatedly produces questions that do more than describe the target.

They ask where the current knowledge is strained, ambiguous, risky, contradictory, fragile, or difficult to interpret.

These questions are valuable, but their ownership is not yet clear.

## Evidence accumulated

### Context

Observed diagnostic questions include:

- What tensions or frictions exist?
- What could be misunderstood without this context?
- What consequences would an incorrect interpretation have?
- What risk exists if we act without resolving an uncertainty?

### Structure

Observed diagnostic questions include:

- What structural tensions exist?
- What parts are strongly coupled?
- Are there circular dependencies?
- Where is a boundary ambiguous?
- What parts overlap?
- What relations are difficult to interpret?

### Domain Vocabulary

Observed diagnostic questions include:

- What ambiguities exist around this term?
- Are there redundant terms?
- Are there overlapping definitions?
- Are there circular definitions?
- Are there incompatible meanings?
- Are there insufficiently defined terms?
- Are there relations whose semantics are unclear?

### Decision Record

Observed diagnostic questions include:

- What criteria were in tension?
- What uncertainties or risks did we accept?
- What could differ from what we expected?
- What consequences could those risks produce?

Decision Record adds an important distinction:

~~~text
recording that a risk was consciously accepted
!=
performing a complete risk analysis
~~~

The first may be intrinsic to the decision. The second may belong to another responsibility.

## Accumulated diagnostic vocabulary

Current recurring patterns include:

- friction;
- coupling;
- ambiguity;
- overlap;
- circular dependency;
- incompatibility;
- consequence;
- risk;
- contradiction;
- insufficient definition;
- difficult-to-interpret relation;
- accepted uncertainty;
- competing criteria.

## Ownership test

For each diagnostic question, ask:

1. Is this necessary to understand the Document's own subject?
2. Is the Document merely recording that a problem exists?
3. Would a deeper investigation require a distinct body of knowledge?
4. Does the same diagnostic pattern recur across unrelated Document types?
5. Would extracting it remove useful semantic pressure from the originating Document?

## Open questions

- Is there a reusable diagnostic vocabulary across Documents?
- Is there a future Document dedicated to tensions, risks, fragility, or review?
- Should Documents retain shallow diagnostic questions while deeper analysis lives elsewhere?
- Are some diagnostic families intrinsic to particular Documents?
- How do we distinguish diagnosis from evaluation or validation?

## Current status

Open research pin.

Keep useful diagnostic questions provisionally where they are discovered. Do not extract them merely for conceptual neatness.
