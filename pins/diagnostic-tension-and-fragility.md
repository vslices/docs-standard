# Diagnostic tension and fragility

## Research question

> Are diagnostic questions refinements owned by each Document, or do they reveal a distinct transversal documentary responsibility that is not yet modeled?

## Observation

Across multiple Document types, useful semantic pressure repeatedly produces questions about friction, ambiguity, risk, contradiction, fragility, difficult cases, or failure.

However, Behavior exposed an important correction:

> A tensioning condition is not automatically diagnostic.

Some difficult conditions are intrinsic to the semantics of the original Document.

The ownership question therefore cannot be decided from vocabulary such as "risk", "failure", "edge case", or "tension" alone.

## Core distinction discovered through Behavior

A provisional test is:

~~~text
If the question asks:
"Under this difficult condition, what should the target do / mean / preserve?"

    -> it may still belong intrinsically to the original Document.

If the question asks:
"What problem, contradiction, fragility, anomaly, or weakness does this knowledge reveal?"

    -> it is a stronger candidate for diagnostic responsibility.
~~~

For Behavior specifically:

~~~text
Behavior
    "given this difficult condition, what must occur?"

Diagnostic / Tension
    "given what we know, what problem or fragility does this reveal?"
~~~

This distinction should be tested against additional Document types.

## Evidence accumulated

### Context

Observed questions include:

- What tensions or frictions exist?
- What could be misunderstood without this context?
- What consequences would an incorrect interpretation have?
- What risk exists if we act without resolving an uncertainty?

Some of these may be intrinsic because they clarify the context needed for correct interpretation.

Others may become diagnostic when they primarily evaluate weakness or exposure rather than describe the context itself.

### Structure

Observed questions include:

- What structural tensions exist?
- What parts are strongly coupled?
- Are there circular dependencies?
- Where is a boundary ambiguous?
- What parts overlap?
- What relations are difficult to interpret?

These are strong diagnostic candidates because they inspect problematic properties of an already represented structure.

### Domain Vocabulary

Observed questions include:

- What ambiguities exist around this term?
- Are there redundant terms?
- Are there overlapping definitions?
- Are there circular definitions?
- Are there incompatible meanings?
- Are there insufficiently defined terms?
- Are there relations whose semantics are unclear?

Ambiguity around one term may be intrinsic to defining that term correctly.

Vocabulary-wide redundancy, overlap, circularity, or incompatibility look more diagnostic because they inspect the health of the vocabulary as a system.

### Decision Record

Observed questions include:

- What criteria were in tension?
- What uncertainties or risks did we accept?
- What could differ from what we expected?
- What consequences could those risks produce?

Decision Record adds an important distinction:

~~~text
recording that a risk was consciously accepted
!=
performing a complete risk analysis
~~~

The first is intrinsic to reconstructing the decision. The second may belong to a diagnostic responsibility.

### Behavior

Behavior provides the strongest ownership correction so far.

Questions such as:

- What unsuccessful outcomes are expected?
- What happens when the behavior is repeated?
- What happens when a time constraint is exceeded?
- What boundary conditions change the expected behavior?
- Can the behavior recover or continue?

may sound diagnostic, but they still define expected behavior under difficult conditions.

By contrast, questions such as:

- Where do documented behaviors contradict each other?
- Which behavior creates systemic fragility?
- Which paths create problematic coupling?
- Where does observed behavior contradict declared behavior?

primarily diagnose problems and are stronger candidates for a separate responsibility.

Behavior also introduces useful distinctions:

~~~text
expected unsuccessful outcome
!=
unexpected failure
!=
boundary condition
!=
diagnostic finding
~~~

### Consistency

Consistency reinforces the distinction introduced by Behavior.

Questions such as:

- What must remain true?
- What constitutes an inconsistency?
- What could appear consistent without actually being consistent?
- What state must be restored after temporary inconsistency?

are intrinsic to Consistency because they define the semantic boundary between coherent and incoherent states.

They are not diagnostic merely because they talk about failure or invalid states.

By contrast, questions such as:

- Where do inconsistencies recur systematically?
- Which parts produce the most consistency violations?
- What pattern explains repeated inconsistency?
- What structural fragility makes the rule frequently break?

primarily diagnose weaknesses in the realized system or represented knowledge.

Consistency therefore strengthens this provisional rule:

~~~text
defines the valid / invalid semantic space
    -> original Document

investigates why reality repeatedly falls into problematic states
    -> stronger Diagnostic / Tension candidate
~~~

## Ownership test

For each candidate question, ask:

1. Does answering this question define the expected semantics of the original target?
2. Is the difficult condition part of the target's normal or expected semantic space?
3. Is the question primarily revealing a weakness, contradiction, anomaly, or fragility?
4. Is the Document recording a known risk, or performing a deeper analysis of that risk?
5. Would extracting the question remove necessary semantic pressure from the originating Document?
6. Does the same diagnostic pattern recur across unrelated Document types?
7. Would a deeper investigation require its own body of evidence and conclusions?

## Open questions

- Is there a reusable diagnostic vocabulary across Documents?
- Is there a future Document dedicated to tensions, risks, fragility, anomalies, or review?
- Should Documents retain intrinsic tension questions while deeper diagnosis lives elsewhere?
- Are some diagnostic families intrinsic to particular Document responsibilities?
- How do we distinguish diagnosis from evaluation, validation, verification, and drift?
- Can one finding be intrinsic to one Document and diagnostic when viewed from another perspective?
